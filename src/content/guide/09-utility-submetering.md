# Utility submetering

Where a property has a water or electricity meter that covers more than one unit,
or an individual meter per unit, RentaPay can turn a monthly reading into a fair,
itemised bill for each tenant, without you needing a separate spreadsheet.

## Setting up a meter

A landlord, manager, or caretaker can register a meter against a property, marking
it as either an individual meter serving one specific unit, or a shared meter
serving several units at once. Each meter has its own billing rate, set by the
landlord, used to convert usage into a cost.

## Submitting a reading

Each billing period, whoever is responsible, often a caretaker on site, submits the
current meter reading. RentaPay compares it to the meter's previous reading to work
out total usage for the period, and flags a reading that looks unusually high or
low compared to that meter's own history, so an obvious typing mistake gets caught
before a bill goes out.

## Splitting a shared meter fairly

For a shared meter covering several units, RentaPay does not simply divide the
total bill evenly. It looks at how many days each unit actually had an active
tenant during that billing period, and splits the cost proportionally, so a unit
that was vacant for part of the month is not charged as if it had been occupied
the whole time.

## Reviewing before you finalise

Before a bill goes out, a landlord or manager can review every unit's calculated
occupied days and amount, and override either one where there is a genuine reason
to, for example a tenant who moved in partway through the month in a way the
system's own dates did not fully capture. Once you are satisfied, finalising the
bill sends it to the affected tenant or tenants as its own invoice, separate from
their rent balance.

## Paying a utility bill

Depending on how you have configured the property, a tenant may pay their utility
bill the same way they pay rent: through M Pesa, with the transaction code
submitted in the app and reviewed and confirmed by the landlord, manager, or
caretaker.

## Who submits a meter reading

Depending on how a property is set up, a meter reading can be submitted by the
landlord, a manager, a caretaker, or in some setups the tenant themselves reading
their own unit meter. Whoever submits it, the reading is timestamped and attached to
the specific meter it was read from, and the resulting bill calculation is based on
the difference between that reading and the previous one, multiplied by the
applicable rate.

## Shared meters and how usage is split

Where a single meter serves more than one unit, such as a shared water meter for a
whole building, RentaPay calculates each unit's share of the total usage and bills
accordingly rather than billing the whole reading to one tenant. The method used to
split a shared reading, such as splitting it evenly across units or based on a
different agreed formula, is configured for that property by the landlord or
manager, and the resulting bill for each affected unit shows how it was calculated
rather than presenting only a final number.

## If a reading looks wrong

A tenant who believes a meter reading or the bill calculated from it is incorrect can
raise it as a dispute the same way they would dispute any other charge, described in
the disputes and complaints section of this guide. Because both the current and
previous readings, and the calculation between them, are kept on record, a disputed
utility bill has a clear, checkable trail to review rather than relying on memory of
what the meter said.
