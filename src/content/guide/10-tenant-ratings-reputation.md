# Tenant ratings and portable reputation

RentaPay lets a landlord, manager, or caretaker rate a tenant on things like
payment reliability and how well they have cared for the unit, and lets a tenant
rate their landlord, manager, or the property itself in return.

## How a tenant's aggregate score works

Every time a landlord rates a tenant, that rating feeds into an aggregate score for
that tenant, broken down by category and by the role of whoever rated them. This
aggregate score is tied to the tenant's own registered email address rather than to
any single landlord's account. That single design choice is what makes the score
portable: a tenant who has built a strong payment history at one property can carry
that same score with them if they move to a new landlord who also uses RentaPay,
entirely at the tenant's own choice, since nothing about it happens automatically
or without the tenant's involvement.

## Sharing a reputation score

A tenant can generate a shareable link to their own aggregate reputation score, and
choose to send that link to a prospective new landlord when inquiring about a
vacant unit, whether that landlord uses RentaPay or not. What is shared through
that link is only the aggregate score and its category breakdown, never any
individual written comment.

## Privacy of written comments

Where a rating includes an optional written comment, that comment is visible only
to the tenant it is about, on their own account, and to RentaPay's own support and
moderation team where relevant. It is never shown to any other landlord, including
within a reputation summary a tenant shares themselves.

## Flagging an unfair rating

A tenant who believes a rating is inaccurate, retaliatory, or abusive can flag it
for review directly from their portal. RentaPay may, at its own discretion, remove
or adjust a rating found to breach the Terms of Service.

## Who calculates a rating, and when

A tenant rating is entered by the landlord, manager, or caretaker responsible for
that tenant's unit, typically after a tenancy ends or at another meaningful point
RentaPay prompts for it, such as after a full year in the unit. RentaPay itself does
not generate a rating automatically from payment history alone; a human on the
landlord's side decides the rating and can, where the feature is enabled, leave a
short explanation alongside it.

## Disputing a rating you believe is unfair

A tenant who believes a rating left about them is inaccurate can raise it as a
dispute through the same disputes flow used for disputing a charge, described
elsewhere in this guide. Raising a dispute does not automatically remove or hide the
rating while it is being looked into; it flags it for review so that both sides of
the disagreement can be considered before any change is made.

## How a landlord's own reputation works

Separately from tenant ratings, a landlord or a property's own reputation can be
affected by patterns such as a high number of unresolved disputes or complaints
raised against units under that landlord's account. This is intended to give a
prospective tenant browsing public listings a general sense of how a landlord tends
to run their properties, in the same spirit that a tenant rating gives a landlord a
general sense of a prospective tenant's history.
