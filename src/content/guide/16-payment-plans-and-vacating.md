# Payment plans and giving notice to vacate

## Payment plans

Sometimes a tenant cannot pay the full month at once, and needs to split it into a
few smaller payments instead. A tenant can propose a payment plan directly from
their portal: a total amount, split into at least two installments, each with its
own amount and due date, and an optional reason. The installment amounts must add
up exactly to the total being requested.

Once submitted, the landlord is notified with the full proposed schedule, and can
approve or decline it from the Payment Plan Requests section of the Finances menu.
A tenant can only have one payment plan request awaiting a decision at a time, so a
landlord is never juggling several competing proposals from the same tenant at
once.

## Giving notice to vacate

When a tenant plans to move out, they can submit a vacating notice from their
portal, giving their intended move out date. This immediately flips their unit's
status to notice given and notifies the landlord, so the landlord knows a vacancy
is coming well before it arrives.

As that date gets closer, RentaPay reminds the tenant automatically, three days
before and again one day before, and each reminder tells the tenant to contact
their landlord directly if the notice was submitted by mistake and they want to
revoke it. On the notice date itself, the tenant's account is deactivated and the
unit flips from notice given to vacant, ready to be listed or assigned to a new
tenant. Deactivating the account does not erase anything: the tenant's payment
history and their portable reputation record are preserved exactly the same way
they are when a landlord removes a tenant directly, not deleted.

If a tenant changes their mind before the notice date arrives, reaching out to
their landlord, manager, or caretaker to revoke the notice keeps the unit marked
as occupied and cancels the countdown entirely.

## Why a payment plan needs the landlord's agreement

A payment plan is not something a tenant can set up unilaterally; it represents an
agreement between a tenant and their landlord, manager, or caretaker about how an
outstanding balance will be paid off over time, typically in smaller instalments
attached to specific dates. Because it changes what is expected of the tenant
compared to the standard due date, the landlord side always has to review and accept
it before it takes effect, the same way a real-world repayment arrangement outside
the app would need both people to agree.

## Giving notice to vacate

A tenant giving notice records it directly within RentaPay, which timestamps exactly
when notice was given, in the same way a written notice letter would but without the
possibility of it being lost or its date disputed later. The notice period itself,
meaning how much advance notice a tenancy requires, is a term of the tenancy
agreement between landlord and tenant, not something RentaPay sets or enforces; the
app records that notice was given and when, and displays it to the landlord side, but
the underlying legal notice period still comes from the lease itself.

## What happens to records after a tenant moves out

Moving out does not delete a tenant's historical payment, deposit, or rating records;
those remain part of the property's history in RentaPay for as long as the platform
generally retains records, described in our Privacy Policy. A vacated unit becomes
available to be marked vacant and re-listed for the next tenant, while the previous
tenant's account and history remain intact and, where relevant, contribute to their
reputation as described in the ratings section of this guide.
