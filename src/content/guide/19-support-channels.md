# How to reach support

RentaPay makes its support contact details visible everywhere they might be
needed, including on screens a user sees before they have even logged in, so
nobody is ever stuck without a way to get help.

## What is shown, and where

Every portal, plus the logged out login and status screens, shows a support email
address and one or more call or WhatsApp numbers. These are not hardcoded; they are
configured centrally and can include several separate lines, for example a primary
number and a backup number, or different lines for different times of day, all
managed from one place so every screen across the whole platform always shows the
same, current information.

## In app chat

Beyond a phone call or a WhatsApp message, RentaPay also has its own in app chat,
letting you reach RentaPay's own support team directly from within the platform,
with a full history of your conversation kept so you never have to repeat yourself
if a different support staff member picks it up. Once your query is resolved, you
may be asked to rate the support you received, and, if that rating has not yet been
given, RentaPay may send a single reminder asking for it.

## Landlord to tenant messaging

Separately from RentaPay's own support chat, a landlord or manager can message a
tenant directly through the same chat feature, where that option has been enabled,
keeping tenant communication in one place rather than scattered across phone calls
and separate messaging apps.

## The public status page

If you are trying to work out whether an issue is something support needs to help
with, or simply a wider, temporary problem on RentaPay's own side, check the public
status page first. It requires no login, refreshes automatically, and breaks down
the platform's health into the specific parts that matter, sign in, payments, email
delivery, push notifications, file storage, background jobs, support chat, and the
public listings page, so you can see exactly what, if anything, is affected before
reaching out.

## Before you reach out

A little context speeds up almost every support conversation. Have the tenant's
unit and property name ready, the approximate time an issue occurred, and, where a
payment is involved, the transaction code in question. This is not required to get
help, but it usually means your first message already has everything support needs
to look into it right away, rather than a back and forth just to gather basic
details.

## In-app chat

The fastest way to reach RentaPay support directly is the chat option inside the
app itself, available from the Messages tab in the tenant portal or the equivalent
support option in the landlord/manager portal. Because it's built into the app,
support already has the context of which account and role is asking, so you
generally don't need to re-explain who you are before getting to the actual
question.

## What to include when reporting a problem

A report that includes which screen you were on, roughly when it happened, and what
you expected to happen instead is resolved faster than "it's not working." If the
issue involves a specific payment, unit, or tenant, naming it directly (rather than
"a payment I made") lets support find the exact record right away instead of
searching for it.

## Checking the status page before reaching out

For anything that looks like the app itself is broken rather than a question about
how a feature works, checking the public status page first, described in its own
section of this guide, can save you a round trip — if the relevant component
already shows "Issue detected," you already know it's a known, wider problem being
looked at, not something specific to you.
