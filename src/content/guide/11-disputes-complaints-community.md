# Disputes, complaints, and the community board

## Disputing a payment

If a tenant believes a payment record is wrong, for example a payment they
submitted was rejected in error, or a confirmed amount does not match what they
actually paid, they can raise a dispute directly from their portal. This creates a
timestamped record both sides can see, and the landlord, manager, or caretaker
responsible for that unit can respond within the same tool. RentaPay does not
decide who is right in a dispute; it simply keeps a clear, shared record of what
was raised and how it was answered, so nothing gets lost in a message thread.

## Raising a complaint or maintenance request

Separately from a payment dispute, a tenant can raise a general complaint or a
maintenance request, describing the issue and, where relevant, attaching a photo.
The landlord, manager, or caretaker responsible sees it immediately and can update
its status as it is worked on, so a tenant is never left wondering whether a
request was actually received.

## When something needs to go further

More serious matters, such as a dispute over a security deposit refund, an alleged
unlawful eviction, or a habitability issue, are civil matters between landlord and
tenant. RentaPay's tools are there to make communication easier and to keep a
record, not to adjudicate; if something cannot be resolved between the two of you
directly, it may need to go to Kenya's courts, rent tribunals, or another dispute
resolution body outside the platform.

## The community board

Where a landlord turns it on, tenants and landlord side users at a property can
post to and reply on a shared community board, for example to share a notice, ask a
question, or arrange something informally between neighbours. A post is visible
only to other users of that same community, not to the wider public, and stays the
responsibility of whoever posted it. RentaPay can remove a post that breaks the
platform's acceptable use rules once it becomes aware of it, though posts are not
reviewed before they go up.

## What can be disputed

A tenant can dispute a specific charge on their account, such as a rent amount they
believe is wrong, a utility bill calculated from a meter reading they don't agree
with, or a fee they don't recognise. A dispute is attached to the specific charge it
concerns rather than filed as a vague general complaint, which keeps the
conversation focused on a concrete, checkable record rather than becoming a broader
disagreement with no clear resolution point.

## How a dispute moves forward

Once raised, a dispute is visible to the landlord, manager, or caretaker responsible
for that unit, who can respond, adjust the charge, or explain why it stands as
recorded. The full back-and-forth, along with the original charge and any supporting
document either side attaches, stays attached to that dispute's record, so anyone
looking at it later, including RentaPay support if asked to help, can see the whole
history rather than just the final outcome.

## The community board

The community board is a shared space visible to tenants and landlords within a
property, intended for posts such as a shared announcement, a notice about scheduled
maintenance, or a general community update. It is not a private messaging channel;
anything posted there is visible to everyone with access to that property's board,
so it should be treated the same way a notice board in a shared building entrance
would be.

## Reporting something on the community board

A post on the community board that is inappropriate, spam, or otherwise against how
the board is meant to be used can be reported, which flags it for a landlord,
manager, or RentaPay's own team to review. Reporting a post does not remove it
automatically; it brings it to the attention of someone who can decide whether it
should be removed.
