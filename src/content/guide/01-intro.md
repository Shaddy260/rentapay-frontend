# Understand RentaPay

## The problem

Collecting rent across even a handful of units usually means chasing bank slips,
cross checking M Pesa messages against a spreadsheet, and hoping nothing falls
through the cracks when a caretaker forgets to note a cash payment. Multiply that
across several properties, a property manager, and a caretaker on the ground, and
reconciliation becomes a part time job on its own. Security deposits get tracked in
a notebook, or not at all. Utility bills for a shared water meter get split by guesswork.
A tenant who paid reliably for two years at one building has no way to show that history
to a new landlord across town.

## What RentaPay does

RentaPay is a single system that landlords, property managers, caretakers, general
managers, and tenants all use to keep rent collection, records, deposits, utility
billing, maintenance, and reporting in one place.

- **Landlords** see every property, every unit, and every payment, paid, pending, or
  late, without waiting on anyone to compile a report, and can record a security
  deposit, bill a shared utility meter, and generate an annual report from the same
  dashboard. A landlord with their own Safaricom Business Paybill or Till can also
  connect it to RentaPay and switch rent collection from manual review to fully
  automatic, with a short one-time setup and no ongoing effort after that. See
  "Automatic rent collection" later in this guide.
- **Property managers and caretakers** record payments as they happen, including
  manual and cash payments, submit meter readings, and handle maintenance and tenant
  contact, all scoped to only the properties they have been given access to.
- **General managers** run the operational side of a landlord's business day to day,
  with sensitive actions protected by their own Operations PIN and reviewable by the
  landlord afterward.
- **Tenants** get a clear way to pay, whether that's a real M-Pesa prompt straight to
  their phone (where their landlord has enabled automatic collection) or paying and
  submitting their payment code for review, plus visibility into their deposit
  status and any utility bill, a way to raise a maintenance request or dispute, and
  a rating history that can travel with them to a new landlord.

## Who it is for

Whether you manage one unit or a hundred, across one building or several, RentaPay
scales with you. You can add properties, units, and staff as your portfolio grows,
without changing how the system works.

## Trust, in short

RentaPay never mixes up whose money is whose. Every payment is tied to a specific
tenant, unit, and property, and only the people you have explicitly given access to,
a manager, caretaker, or general manager you have invited, can see or act on your
properties. Rent itself is paid directly from tenant to landlord by M Pesa. RentaPay
never holds, takes custody of, or has control over that money at any point. See the
Privacy Policy for full detail on how your data is handled, and the Terms of Service
for exactly what RentaPay is and is not.

## Who builds and operates RentaPay

RentaPay is operated as a single platform serving every account on it - landlords,
property managers, caretakers, general managers, tenants, prospective tenants
browsing public listings, and Brand Ambassadors all use the same underlying system,
just with different views and permissions suited to their role. There is no
separate "enterprise" version with different reliability guarantees; every account
runs on the same infrastructure, gets the same security measures, and sees new
features the moment they ship rather than on a staggered rollout.

## The roles in one place

A **landlord** owns one or more properties and has full visibility and control over
everything within their account: units, tenants, payments, deposits, maintenance,
and reports. A **property manager** is typically given similar day-to-day access on
a landlord's behalf, often across several properties. A **caretaker** usually has a
narrower, more operational set of permissions focused on day-to-day tasks like
confirming payments and logging maintenance updates rather than financial reporting
or account settings. A **general manager** oversees several landlord accounts at
once, common for a management company. A **tenant** sees only their own unit,
balance, payment history, and the maintenance and messaging tools relevant to their
own tenancy. A **Brand Ambassador** operates in a separate portal focused on
referring prospective landlords and tracking their own commission, without access
to any landlord's actual tenant or financial data.

## Why RentaPay exists

Before a tool like this, a lot of rent management in the Kenyan market has
historically happened across a mix of paper receipts, WhatsApp messages, and
memory - workable, but easy for a specific payment, a specific conversation, or a
specific promise to get lost, especially once a dispute arises and everyone is
trying to reconstruct what actually happened months later. RentaPay's core value is
simply keeping a single, timestamped, shared record that both a landlord and a
tenant can point to and trust, rather than replacing the underlying relationship or
legal agreement between them.
