# Using RentaPay on any device

RentaPay is designed mobile first, since most tenants, and a good number of
caretakers and property managers, use it primarily from a phone. It works the same
way in any modern browser, on a phone, a tablet, or a desktop computer, and there
is no separate app to install to get the full set of features.

## Photos that respond to how you browse them

Wherever you browse a set of unit photos, whether in the unit editor as a landlord,
or in the full screen viewer as a tenant or a prospective tenant on a public
listing, the same smooth, focus based photo strip is used: scroll through the
thumbnails and whichever photo is centered scales up and brightens, while its
neighbours settle back, so browsing several photos feels natural on a touch screen
without needing to tap through them one at a time.

## No installation required

Because RentaPay runs entirely in the browser, there is nothing to download from an
app store and nothing that needs to be kept updated manually. Simply visiting
RentaPay's own web address, or opening a home screen icon if one has been added
previously, is enough to get the current version of the app every time, as
described in the previous section.

## Staying signed in

When you sign in on a device, RentaPay keeps you signed in for seven days before
asking you to sign in again, regardless of whether you are on a phone, tablet, or
computer. Signing out manually, or changing your password, ends that session
immediately rather than waiting for the seven days to pass. If you use RentaPay on
more than one device, for example a phone during the day and a computer at home,
each device keeps its own separate session, so signing out on one does not sign you
out on the other.

## Scrolling, touch gestures, and why the app should always feel responsive

RentaPay's mobile layout is built so that scrolling and touch gestures are handled
natively by your browser wherever possible, rather than being intercepted by the
app's own code. This matters because a page that fights your finger, feels laggy, or
occasionally refuses to scroll at all is one of the most common complaints about
poorly built mobile web apps. If you ever notice a screen that seems to resist
scrolling, feels unusually slow to respond to a swipe, or shows content tucked
underneath the bottom navigation bar, that is a bug worth reporting through the Help
option, since it is not how the app is intended to behave on any device.

## Notches, home indicators, and safe areas

On phones with a notch, a punch-hole camera, or a home indicator bar at the bottom of
the screen (most modern iPhones and many recent Android phones), RentaPay
automatically leaves extra space around the bottom navigation bar and other
fixed-position elements so they never sit underneath that hardware feature or get
covered by it. You should never need to awkwardly angle your thumb around a notch or
a home indicator bar to tap a button in RentaPay; if you find yourself doing that on
a particular screen, it is worth reporting.

## Data usage

RentaPay is built to be usable on a modest mobile data connection. Photos you upload,
such as a unit photo or a payment proof screenshot, are the largest single thing the
app transfers; everything else, including your balance, payment history, and
messages, is lightweight text and loads quickly even on a slower connection. There is
currently no dedicated "low data mode" toggle within the app; if data usage is a
concern, uploading photos over Wi-Fi where available will make the biggest
difference.
