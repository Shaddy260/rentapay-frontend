# Security deposits

Many landlords collect a security deposit at the start of a tenancy, refundable
later depending on the condition the unit is left in. RentaPay gives you a clean,
dedicated way to record this, kept entirely separate from rent.

## Recording a deposit

When you onboard a new tenant, or at any point afterward from that tenant's unit
screen, you can record whether they paid a security deposit and how much. This
figure is stored on its own; it is never added to the tenant's rent balance, is
never available to be drawn down against unpaid rent automatically, and is never
treated by RentaPay as money the platform itself holds. A tenant can see their own
recorded deposit amount and its current status at any time from their own portal.

## Settling a deposit at move out

When a tenancy ends, you or your manager settles the deposit by recording one of
three outcomes:

- **Refunded**, meaning the full amount was returned to the tenant.
- **Partially refunded**, meaning some amount was returned and some was withheld,
  together with a written reason for what was withheld and why.
- **Forfeited**, meaning none of the deposit was returned.

This is a record keeping feature, not a payment feature. RentaPay does not decide,
verify, or adjudicate whether a deduction was fair or lawful, and does not itself
transfer any money as part of this process. What counts as a lawful deduction from
a security deposit is governed by the tenancy agreement between you and your
tenant, and by applicable Kenyan law, not by anything RentaPay displays.

## Why keep it separate from rent

Keeping a deposit as its own record, rather than folding it into a tenant's general
balance, means a tenant can never be shown as owing money simply because a deposit
has not been formally settled yet, and a landlord always has a clean, dated history
of what was collected and what happened to it at the end of every tenancy across
their whole portfolio.

## Why the deposit amount is recorded separately from rent

A security deposit is tracked as its own record, separate from monthly rent
payments, precisely because it behaves differently: it is paid once at the start of
a tenancy, is not treated as available rent income, and is expected to eventually be
refunded, partially refunded, or forfeited rather than simply accumulating like a
running rent balance. Keeping it as a distinct record means a landlord's monthly rent
collection figures are never accidentally inflated by a deposit payment, and a
tenant's deposit history remains clearly visible on its own at any point in the
tenancy, not folded into their regular payment ledger.

## Partial refunds and stated reasons

When a tenancy ends, a landlord can record a deposit as refunded in full, refunded
partially with a stated reason for the amount withheld, such as damage or unpaid
utility charges, or forfeited entirely with a reason. Recording a reason alongside a
partial refund or forfeiture is intended to keep the record honest and useful for
both sides; a tenant can see exactly what was withheld and why, rather than simply
seeing a smaller number with no explanation, which is often where a deposit
disagreement starts in the first place.

## If you disagree with how a deposit was handled

A tenant who disagrees with how their deposit was refunded, partially refunded, or
forfeited can raise it through the same disputes flow described in the disputes and
complaints section of this guide. Because RentaPay keeps a timestamped record of the
original deposit amount, the date it was recorded as returned or withheld, and any
reason given, that record is the starting point for resolving the disagreement,
whether that resolution happens directly between landlord and tenant or, where
necessary, elsewhere.
